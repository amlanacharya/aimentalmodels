Statistics for AI Engineers

Understand the probability, training objectives, sampling, uncertainty, and metrics that appear everywhere in modern AI systems.

Chapter 1

Foundations: describe uncertainty before you model it

Four ideas carry most of the load: center, spread, dependence, and conditional probability.

Mean, variance, covariance

Mean says where data is centered. Variance says how much observations wander around that center. Covariance says whether two variables move together.

mean = average(x)\nvariance = average((x − mean)²)\ncovariance ≈ do x and y move together?
Mental model: a mean without spread is incomplete. Two systems can have the same average latency and wildly different reliability.
normalizationPCAmonitoringeval stability

Conditional probability & Bayes

Bayes is simply disciplined belief updating: prior belief + strength of new evidence → posterior belief.

P(H | E) ∝ P(E | H) × P(H)
Mental model: rare events stay rare unless evidence is strong enough to overcome the base rate. “95% sensitivity” is not “95% probability the diagnosis is true.”
fraudanomaly detectionBayesian searchconfidence reasoning

Independence

Many confidence calculations quietly assume observations are independent. Ten questions derived from one PDF are not equivalent to ten independent sources of evidence.

independent: P(A | B) = P(A)
Mental model: count independent units, not just rows. 1,000 messages from 5 users can be much less informative than 1,000 messages from 1,000 users.

Correlation ≠ causation

Telemetry is great at finding associations and terrible at proving why they happen. Power users may both use your AI feature more and retain more.

Mental model: before claiming “feature X improved outcome Y,” ask what else could explain both. Randomization is the cleanest answer when feasible.
Chapter 2

Training: why cross-entropy actually works

The fastest route to understanding model training is to connect likelihood → log likelihood → cross-entropy → gradient descent.

Observed training data→Make it more probable→Negative log-likelihood→Cross-entropy

Maximum Likelihood Estimation

MLE chooses parameters that make the data you observed as probable as possible.

θ* = argmaxθ P(data | θ)

For next-token training, every update pushes probability toward the token that actually occurred.

Mental model: model training is not “memorize labels.” It is repeatedly reallocating probability mass toward observed targets.

Cross-entropy

For a one-hot correct token, the loss is essentially the negative log-probability assigned to that token.

loss = −log P(correct token)
P(correct)Interpretation
0.95small penalty
0.50meaningful penalty
0.01very large penalty

KL divergence

KL compares probability distributions. It is not a true geometric distance, but it answers a practical question: how much did the policy distribution move?

H(P,Q) = H(P) + KL(P || Q)
Mental model: with a fixed target distribution, minimizing cross-entropy also minimizes KL. In policy optimization, KL can be used to constrain how far an updated policy moves.

Bias, variance & overfitting

Underfit: training and validation both poor. Overfit: training improves while validation degrades.

Mental model: the same overfitting problem happens when you tune prompts or RAG settings repeatedly against one eval set.
Chapter 3

Inference: logits become choices

An LLM produces scores, converts them to a categorical probability distribution, then a decoding strategy decides how to choose the next token.

Logits→Softmax→Token probabilities→temperature / top-k / top-p→next token

Probability distributions

Every next-token step is a categorical distribution over the vocabulary. “Paris = 0.61” means the model assigned 61% of the probability mass to that candidate in that context.

softmax(zᵢ) = exp(zᵢ) / Σ exp(zⱼ)
Mental model: probabilities are relative. Adding or changing one logit changes the normalized probabilities of others.

Temperature, top-k, top-p

Temperature reshapes the distribution. Top-k truncates to k candidates. Top-p keeps the smallest set whose cumulative mass reaches p.

softmax(logits / T)
Mental model: temperature below 1 sharpens; above 1 flattens. It is a distribution-shaping parameter, not a mystical creativity setting.

Chapter 5

Evals: a score without uncertainty is incomplete

Most AI evaluation errors come from treating a noisy sample score as absolute truth.

Sampling & confidence intervals

Uncertainty usually shrinks roughly with 1 / √n. To halve the uncertainty, you need about 4× the samples.

standard error ∝ 1 / √n
Mental model: “85% vs 82%” is meaningless until you know sample size, pairing, and uncertainty.

Hypothesis testing / A-B testing

Define the metric, hypothesis, population, and stopping rule before looking at results. Then measure effect size + uncertainty, not p-value alone.

Mental model: statistical significance answers “probably not noise.” Engineering significance asks “worth the latency, money, and complexity?”
Illustrative 82% accuracy. Approximate 95% interval using a normal approximation to a binomial proportion.

Precision, recall, F1

Precision: of what I flagged, how much was real? Recall: of what was real, how much did I catch?

precision = TP / (TP + FP)\nrecall = TP / (TP + FN)\nF1 = harmonic mean(precision, recall)
Mental model: when classes are imbalanced, accuracy can look excellent while the system is useless.

ROC-AUC vs PR-AUC

ROC-AUC is useful for ranking performance across thresholds, but with rare positives a Precision–Recall view often reveals minority-class behavior more clearly.

Mental model: choose metrics from error costs and class balance, not habit.
